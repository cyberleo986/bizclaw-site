1. The "features instead of outcomes" headline

The #1 killer. The headline tells me what the product does, not what it does for me. Visitors don't care about your features until they trust you'll solve their problem.

Before

"AI-powered customer support platform"

After

"Cut your support tickets 60% in 30 days — without hiring another agent."

The rewrite formula: [Specific outcome] + [Timeframe] + [What you don't have to do anymore].

2. The "we" instead of "you" voice

If your hero copy has the word "we" more than 3 times in the first 200 words, the page is about you, not the visitor. Every "we built" is a chance to swap to "you get".

Before

"We've built a platform that helps teams like yours. We believe in..."

After

"Your team ships faster when you stop context-switching. Get back 4 hours/week with..."

3. The "trust us" social proof

"Trusted by 10,000+ customers" means nothing. "Used by 3 YC-backed startups to hit $100k MRR in 90 days" means everything. Specificity is the only thing that converts in social proof.

Before

"Loved by teams worldwide"

After

"Used daily by 14,000+ product teams. 4.9/5 on G2 from 800+ reviews."

The rewrite formula: [Specific number] + [Specific segment] + [Specific source].

4. The buried CTA

If a visitor has to scroll to find the "Get started" button, the page is broken. The primary CTA should be visible above the fold on every screen size.

Second-most-common issue: the CTA text says "Sign up" or "Learn more". Both are friction words. Use the action + outcome.

[Sign up] [Get a demo]

5. The no-pricing page

"Contact us for pricing" is a 60% bounce trigger. The visitor has done the work to figure out if they're a fit — making them email you to find out the price is a tax on their intent.

The exception: if your ACV is $50k+ and you genuinely do consultative sales. For everyone else, put the price on the page.

6. The "about us" above the fold

If the first thing a visitor sees is your origin story ("Founded in 2019 by two engineers from Google..."), they bounce. Nobody cares about you until they trust you'll solve their problem.

Move the founder story to a footer link called "Our story". Keep the hero focused on the visitor's pain.

7. The 5-tap signup

Every form field you add drops conversion 7-11% (per unbounce's industry data). If your signup takes name + email + company + role + phone + company size, you're losing 35-50% of the visitors who'd otherwise convert.

Before (8 fields)

First name, last name, work email, company, role, company size, phone, password

After (2 fields)

Work email, password

Ask for the rest after they're in the product and see value.

8. The "screenshot-only" hero

Product screenshots are good. Screenshots with no context are noise. A visitor doesn't know what they're looking at.

Pair every screenshot with a one-line caption: "Build your first landing page in 60 seconds" or "Track your team's velocity across 4 metrics".

9. The features grid without pain framing

"Unlimited projects, real-time collaboration, advanced permissions" — these are table stakes for the segment you serve. They don't differentiate. The visitor can get these from 8 other tools.

Re-frame every feature as a problem it solves:

Before

✓ Real-time collaboration

After

✓ Stop emailing "v3-final-final.docx" — your whole team edits one document, sees every change, leaves inline comments

10. The no-objection-handling FAQ

Every SaaS visitor has 3-5 specific objections: "Is my data secure?", "What if I cancel?", "How long does setup take?", "Do you integrate with X?". If your page doesn't answer them, they leave and Google them.

Put a 5-8 question FAQ above the footer. Answer each in 2 sentences, max.

11. The "we're different" without proof

"Unlike other tools, we..." — the visitor is now waiting to hear the proof. If you don't deliver in the next sentence, you've lost them.

The formula: "Unlike [specific competitor type], we [specific differentiator] because [specific reason]."

Before

"Unlike other CRMs, we focus on what matters."

After

"Unlike Salesforce, we don't charge per seat. You get the whole CRM for $99/mo flat — because we built it for teams of 5-50, not 5000."

12. The no-urgency CTA

If your CTA says "Start your free trial" and there's no specific reason to start today, the visitor bookmark-tabs you and forgets. Give them a reason.

Valid urgency triggers: limited-time pricing, expiring beta access, live cohort starting, founder-led onboarding slot. Avoid fake countdown timers (they hurt trust more than they help conversion).

Before

[Start your free trial →]

After

[Start free — get 1:1 onboarding from our founder this week →]
