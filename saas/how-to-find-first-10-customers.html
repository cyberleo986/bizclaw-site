Channel 1: Where are your first 10 customers already talking?

Before you write a single line of cold email, you need to know where the demand already exists. There are exactly three places to look:

  1. Communities where the problem is a recurring topic (HN, Reddit, IndieHackers, niche Slack/Discord, industry forums)
  2. Complaint threads about your competitors (search "competitor name alternative" on every platform)
  3. Public wishlists and "I wish this existed" posts (Twitter, HN Ask, Reddit r/SomebodyMakeThis)

The mistake most founders make: they join these communities and start pitching. That gets you banned. The right move: be useful first, sell second.

1Map your "demand landscape"

Spend 60 minutes building a list of 30-50 specific URLs — HN threads, Reddit posts, Twitter complaints — where someone recently articulated the pain you solve. Save them in a spreadsheet with columns: URL, Platform, Author, Pain quote (1 sentence), Replied? (Y/N), Date.

This list is your outreach plan. Every URL is a warm touch. No cold calls, no guessing, no spam.

Channel 2: The "useful reply" outreach (highest conversion, lowest cost)

Once you have your 30-50 URL list, the next move is to reply on those threads with something genuinely helpful. Not "Hey, I built X, check it out." That's spam.

The template that works:

  1. Read the original post carefully.
  2. Make a specific observation they didn't already know.
  3. Suggest a fix they could try in the next 10 minutes (a free tool, a different workflow, a script).
  4. Only mention your product if it's the natural next step after the free fix.

Example, for a Roast My LP type tool:

"Saw your post about [specific thing they built]. I went to your landing page — the headline leads with the feature, not the outcome. Try rewriting it as 'Get [outcome] without [pain]' and see if your conversion rate moves 10-20%. I built a tool that does this kind of roast in 60 seconds if you want the full breakdown."

Reply to 10 threads over 5 days. Expect 2-3 founders to click through. Expect 1 to buy. That's the entire funnel.

2Write 3 reply templates per thread, not 1

For each thread you reply to, draft 3 versions: one short (3 sentences), one long (with a code example or screenshot), and one "the contrarian take" (disagrees with the OP in a useful way). Rotate between them so you don't look like a bot.

Channel 3: Targeted cold email (use sparingly, only with research)

Cold email gets a 1-3% reply rate when it's generic and a 15-25% rate when it's specific. The difference is the research.

The cold email formula that actually works for the first 10 customers:

  • Subject line: "[Specific thing I noticed about your company]" — never "Quick question" or "Loved your product"
  • First line: "I saw [specific post they wrote / specific feature they shipped / specific customer complaint they're handling]" — prove you did the homework
  • Second line: "That suggests [specific problem they probably have]" — connect the dot for them
  • Third line: "I built [your product] for exactly this. Try it free here: [link]. If it doesn't help, no worries."
  • PS: "[One specific offer — first 5 customers get it free, or 50% off, or 1:1 setup help]"

You should send no more than 5-10 of these per day. Each one takes 10-15 minutes of research. That's 1-2 hours of focused work daily.

Where to find the addresses: Hunter.io, Apollo.io, the company's /humans.txt, or just the founder's public Twitter/Bluesky DM. For 10 customers, you need 100-300 targeted emails sent over 2-4 weeks.
